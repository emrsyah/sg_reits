## Key Metrics to Evaluate REIT Performance
Standard financial metrics like Net Income or Earnings Per Share (EPS) may not always provide a complete picture of a Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) due to the impact of non-cash property depreciation. To better evaluate this asset class, investors can look to a specialized set of sector-specific metrics. 

These indicators serve as a practical guide to analyzing property-level income, actual cash flow distribution capabilities, and leverage structures, helping to form a more tailored framework for assessing a REIT's operational stability and dividend sustainability.

### Property Yield
> Net Property Yield = Net Property Income / Total Portfolio Property Value
Measures actual net cash return generated by the properties relative to their valuation and cost. It reflects what the properties earn, reflecting rental efficiency and property management. A higher property yield indicates stronger asset-level earning power independent of financing choices.

### Cost of Debt
> Cost of Debt = Total Annual Interest Expense / Total Outstanding Debt
Represents the effective interest rate the REIT pays on its borrowings. In high-rate environments, tracking this helps investors assess refinancing risks and understand how rising interest obligations might impact future net income.

### Gearing Ratio
> Gearing Ratio = Total Debt / Total Deposited Assets
Singapore REITs have a regulated gearing ratio limit of 50% (only allowed to borrow up to 50% of their total assets) and currently on the average gearing ratio of x%. This safety margin ensures S-REITs remain well-capitalized against structural property devaluations.

### Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio
> P/B Ratio =. Current Market Price / NAV per unit
A valuation multiplier indicating whether a REIT is trading at a premium (P/B > 1.0) or a discount (P/B < 1.0) relative to its net holdings. Deep discounts can signal market distress, while premiums suggest high asset quality and strong investor conviction.

### Net Asset Value (NAV)
> NAV = GAV - Total Liabilities
The total value of equity attributable to unitholders after all liabilities and debt obligations are deducted from the trust's gross assets. It serves as the baseline calculation for identifying intrinsic per-unit value.

### Gross Asset Value (GAV)
> GAV = Market Value of Investment Properties + Cash & Cash Equivalent + Other Assets
The cumulative fair market value of all underlying real estate assets and liquid cash resources managed by the trust, before subtracting any leverage. GAV is the core denominator used by regulators to verify leverage compliance.

### Funds from Operations (FFO)
> FFO = Net Income + Depreciation & Amortization - Gains on Sale
The standard operating cash flow metric for the real estate sector. Because accounting rules mandate heavy non-cash depreciation on real estate, FFO adds this depreciation back to present a clearer picture of recurring operational profitability.

### Distribution Per Unit (DPU)
> DPU = Total Distributable Income / Total Unitshareholder
The specific dollar amount allocated to each individual share unit. Normalized across the entire unitholder base, DPU is the single most vital metric tracking actual payout growth trends over time.

### Distribution Yield (aka Dividend Yield)
> Distribution Yield = Annualized DPU / Current Market Price
The annualized percentage return an investor receives relative to the current market entry price of the unit. It allows investors to bench market returns directly against risk-free assets like government bonds.

No single metric can tell the full story of a REIT's investment potential. Evaluating a REIT effectively requires analyzing these financial indicators collectively—such as balancing a high distribution yield against its gearing ratio and cost of debt to ensure the payout is sustainable. 

Furthermore, these figures gain meaningful context only when compared against sector peers with similar asset classes, allowing investors to isolate outstanding performers from broader market trends and make truly informed investment decisions.
