What I did, in one slide

Goal: figure out what data we can reliably promise users for every SGX REIT, then design the schema around that evidence — not around hope.

Step 1

Parse a deliberately diverse sample

22 annual reports via LlamaParse (agentic tier), chosen to cover every sector, structure & currency: retail, office, logistics, data centres, hospitality, healthcare, dorms; SGD/USD/EUR/GBP/RMB/JPY/INR; healthy and distressed trusts.

Step 2

Audit every report section-by-section

For each trust: what's disclosed per-property vs portfolio-only, under what label, on what basis, with page citations. The last 6 reports were picked adversarially to try to break the schema.

Step 3

Synthesize the schema + ingestion plan

Merged: the audit evidence + colleague's draft workbook + her review feedback + our prod sgx_* conventions → sgx_reit_schema_v2.md and ingestion_plan.md.

The single most important takeaway

Disclosure is three-tiered: a universal core every trust publishes, a mixed middle where availability AND meaning vary per trust, and a long tail of sector-specific metrics. The schema mirrors this exactly: typed columns → qualified nullable columns → flexible facts table. "Null" in our DB means "the trust doesn't disclose it" — never "we failed to extract it".

Tier 1 — Universal

Audited Portfolio Statement, financial highlights, NPI/DPU/NAV, MAS capital metrics, debt maturity, distributions, fee formulas, unitholder stats.
→ typed screener columns; what the API always answers.

Tier 2 — Mixed

Per-property revenue & NPI, property trade mix, acquisition dates, tenant sales. Availability AND meaning vary by trust.
→ nullable columns + qualifiers (basis, scope, ownership basis, disclosure status).

Tier 3 — Long tail

RevPAU, shopper traffic, kW IT load, beds, outlet sales, VIP members, PML, rent reversion, covenant waivers.
→ facts table with scope/basis — no migration when a report invents a metric.

Tricky findings — same word, different meaning

These are the cases that make naive cross-REIT comparison silently wrong, and why the schema carries qualifier columns everywhere. Click each card to reveal the example.

"Gross revenue" ≠ rent click

The headline revenue number is built differently per trust.

KORE US: 28% of gross revenue is "recoveries income" — tenants reimbursing taxes/opex, not rent.
Stoneweg: €44M of recoverable service charges inside revenue.
CICT footnotes it explicitly: gross revenue = GRI + car park + other income.
Schema answer: sgx_reit_revenue_components stores base_rental / turnover_rent / service_charge / recoveries / car_park separately.

Sasseur has no NPI at all click

One trust's income model makes our core metric undefined.

Sasseur (China outlet malls) earns EMA rental income: fixed rent escalating 3%/yr + 4.0–5.5% of each outlet's tenant sales. The Entrusted Manager pays all property expenses — so NPI doesn't exist.
But disclosure is rich: per-outlet EMA income, outlet sales, trade mix, shopper traffic, VIP members.
Schema answer: income_model enum gates which columns can exist; ema_rental_income + outlet_sales columns.

Consolidated vs attributable click

Keppel REIT's NPI is two very different numbers.

Consolidated NPI = S$215.9M. Add its one-third JV stakes in MBFC/One Raffles Quay etc. → attributable NPI = S$381.4M. Leverage is also stated on the MAS proportionate basis. Suntec adds a third flavor: JV buildings report "net income contribution".
Schema answer: npi_basis ∈ consolidated | attributable | net_income_contribution; held_via + ownership_pct on properties.

Eight different denominators click

"Top tenant = 8%" — eight percent of what?

Bases found in the wild: % of GRI (CICT, excl. turnover rent), gross revenue (MLT), rental income (First REIT), headline rent (Stoneweg), cash rental income (KORE), committed monthly gross rent (Keppel REIT), NLA, and Sasseur's mix is % of tenant sales.
Schema answer: every percentage row has a mandatory basis enum — comparisons must be basis-aware or they're rejected.

"Term of lease" is a trap click

The exact phrase colleague flagged — confirmed in the data.

In a Portfolio Statement, "Term of lease" = the REIT's land lease (MLT writes "30+30 years"), not tenant leases. Acquisition dates appear as "Date of legal completion" (MLT), "Acquisition date" (CLAR/First REIT), or "Purchase Price in 2005" prose (CICT). And acquisitions are often phased: Rock Square 51% (2018) + 49% (2020).
Schema answer: alias dictionary for extraction + sgx_reit_property_events (multiple date/stake/price events per property).

Null has three meanings click

Daiwa House taught us missing ≠ missing.

Daiwa discloses NPI for all 19 properties but withholds gross revenue on single-tenant warehouses — tenants refused consent (confidentiality clause). That's different from "not disclosed" and very different from "extraction failed".
Schema answer: revenue_disclosure ∈ disclosed | confidential | not_disclosed.

The distressed REIT click

Manulife US — the stress test for bad times.

DPU = 0 since 2023; going-concern audit qualification; covenant relaxations with sunset dates (80% gearing cap expires 30 Jun 2026); lender-mandated disposal targets (83% achieved); sponsor loan with a 21.16% exit premium; −6.1% rent reversion.
Schema answer: sgx_reit_covenants table + going_concern_uncertainty flag — arguably the most newsworthy data a REIT product can serve.

One trust, three P&L models click

CLAS Ascott doesn't even say "NPI".

CLAS reports "gross profit" split across master leases (fixed rent, zero staff costs), MCMGI (minimum guaranteed income), and management contracts (full hotel P&L with S$133.7M staff costs inside direct expenses). ~103 properties, zero per-property financials.
Schema answer: segments table gains dimension='contract_type'; income_model='mixed' at trust level with per-property overrides.

Colleague's feedback → what the evidence says

Her review raised three points. Each was tested against the corpus rather than debated.

Confirmed — and worse than flagged

1. "Terms like tenure, purchase/effective date, manager must be captured precisely"

Verified across 21 reports: the same concept hides under different labels, and near-identical labels mean different things. "Manager" splits into at least 7 roles (REIT manager, property manager, operator, master lessee, trustee, sponsor, entrusted manager) — CICT alone has 3 property managers by asset class.

Schema response: seeded alias dictionary for the extractor prompts + role-typed sgx_reit_entities + land_tenure_* family + acquisition events table (phased stakes are common).
Half-confirmed

2. "Tenant composition has two sets — take property level, aggregate to REIT level"

Both sets exist only for CICT (26 per-mall trade-mix tables, same basis → aggregation is verifiable) and Sasseur/CLCT partially. The other ~18 trusts publish portfolio-level only — even FCT, a pure retail REIT. And the bases differ across trusts.

Schema response: one table, two scopes — sgx_reit_tenant_mix(scope ∈ property|segment|portfolio, basis NOT NULL, is_derived). Property→REIT aggregation becomes a validation bonus where both levels exist, not the pipeline.
Strongly confirmed — with one boundary

3. "GRI is property potential; NPI reflects management choices; standardize computed metrics on our side"

Exactly what the data shows. NPI expense composition varies wildly: utilities = 35% of Ascendas's property expenses; doubtful-receivables allowance = 36% of Keppel DC's; marketing is retail-only; KORE buries non-cash straight-lining (reported NPI +3.0%, adjusted +0.3%). The boundary: standardized NPI is only computable at REIT level — no trust discloses per-property expenses.

Schema response: store raw audited revenue_components + expense_components + accounting_adjustments; compute standardized metrics in the API layer (same place prod already computes yield/P-NAV). Per-property: gross revenue (comparable) + as-reported NPI (sparse, flagged) only.

The sgx_reit_* schema — interactive browser

Postgres, keyed on symbol → joins our existing sgx_companies / sgx_daily_data. Click a table to see its columns and the evidence that earned it. Full DDL in sgx_reit_schema_v2.md.

Design rules
  • Never duplicate prod: price/market cap stay in sgx_companies/sgx_daily_data
  • Computed metrics (yield, P/NAV, standardized NPI) live in the API layer, never as stored columns
  • Every % carries a basis; every fact carries source_page provenance
  • As-disclosed, never imputed — nulls are structural and documented

Stress-test verdict

The final 6 reports were chosen to break this design (business trust, TMK structures, partial stakes, distress, convention centre, self-storage). Result: zero structural rewrites — every break was absorbed by a qualifier column, one small table (covenants, developments), or an extraction rule. The three-layer shape held across 21 trusts, 6 income models, 7 currencies.

Ingestion plan — cheap, routed, validated

Lesson learned the hard way: agentic-parsing every page exhausted 4 free plans for 22 documents. The fix: only ~50–70 of ~250 pages per report contain schema data, and section headers are near-standardized — so route. Click each stage.

Zero-cost bake-off (we already own the ground truth)

Our 22 agentic-parsed reports double as a benchmark: sample ~30 hard pages, run LiteParse (LlamaIndex's newly open-sourced engine), Docling, Mistral OCR 3, and Reducto (starter credits) against the baseline, and score on the only metric that matters — do the extractors produce identical validated rows? That decides the parse ladder without spending a credit.
