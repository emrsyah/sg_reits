Typed core → qualified satellites → facts & chunks

The schema is not one big table of REIT numbers. It is a three-layer shape: a typed screener core for what every trust discloses, qualifier-carrying satellite tables for what most trusts disclose differently, and a facts/chunks long tail for everything else. Click any table to open it in the explorer.

The relational spine

Everything keys on text symbol referencing prod's sgx_companies — no new integer IDs for trusts, no duplication of price/volume/market-cap. Properties get a surrogate key because names recur and stakes phase in over years.

Ten questions the 20-report corpus settled

Each design decision traces to a question we asked of the parsed reports. The answers are rarely the convenient ones — six income models, no shared NPI definition, nine percentage denominators — and the schema absorbs them with qualifiers instead of pretending they don't exist.

Six adversarial reports vs. the schema

After v2 was drafted, six deliberately awkward FY2025 reports were parsed against it: a business trust, a Japan TMK, partial-stake data centres, a distressed restructuring, a convention centre + JV stack, and strip retail + self-storage.

Every break was absorbed by a qualifier column, one small table, or an extraction rule — zero structural rewrites across 20 trusts, healthy and distressed.

Expected fill rates, 20-report corpus

Nullable means "this trust doesn't disclose it" — a structural fact the API documents, not a parsing failure. Occupancy is structurally null for master-lease trusts; NPI does not exist under Sasseur's EMA model.

Reading the gaps: per-property NPI improved from 4/14 to 7/20 once Daiwa (all 19 properties!), CLINT and Suntec joined the corpus — but it stays a nullable, never-imputed column because no trust discloses per-property expenses. A standardized NPI is only computable at REIT level, from the component tables.
