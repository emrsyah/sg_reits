## Types of REITs
Investing in Singapore Real Estate Investment Trusts (S-REITs) requires an assessment of factors beyond dividend yield, including asset quality, sectoral fundamentals, and growth strategies.

Singapore REITs market is diversified into various sub-sectors and asset classes, each with distinct risk-return profiles.

### Office
Office (or commerical) REITs own and lease commercial properties to corporate tenants. These trusts are typically sensitive to economic cycles, as demand for office space correlates with business expansion and contraction. Lease structures in this sector are generally longer-term compared to retail, providing a degree of income visibility, though they remain subject to market rental rate fluctuations upon lease renewal.

### Retail
Retail REITs manage shopping malls, which generally fall into two categories: prime malls located in central or urban areas and suburban malls serving residential heartlands. Revenue is primarily derived from rental income, often structured with a base rent and a component tied to the tenants' gross turnover. These properties are sensitive to consumer spending patterns and retail trends.

### Industrial
Industrial REITs hold assets such as factories, logistics warehouses, and distribution centres. This sector often exhibits higher dividend yields, which can be attributed to the shorter land lease profiles associated with industrial properties in Singapore. And just like offices, industrial REITs are largely affected by economic conditions and cycles. At the same time, this sector experienced increased demand driven by the growth of e-commerce and regional supply chain activities.

### Healthcare
Healthcare REITs own hospitals and nursing homes, and generate income by leasing them to healthcare tenants. The healthcare industry is defensive in nature and relies on non-discretionary spending to keep its operations running. Properties are often leased via long-term master lease agreements, reflecting the high capital expenditure required for medical infrastructure. These assets typically demonstrate high barriers to entry and lower susceptibility to sudden market oversupply.

### Hospitality
Hospitality REITs own hotels and serviced apartments. Their income structures commonly include a fixed rent component from master leases with operators, supplemented by variable income linked to hotel performance and room occupancy rates. The sector is highly sensitive to economic cycles and external shocks like pandemics or global events that impact travel.

### Data Centre
Data Centre REITs specialize in facilities designed to house critical IT infrastructure, including servers and networking equipment. These properties require significant investment in specialized features such as advanced cooling systems, high-capacity power supplies, and high-security measures. The sector is supported by structural demand for digital infrastructure, though it is capital-intensive and subject to evolving regulatory requirements regarding power usage and sustainability.

### Diversified
Diversified REITs maintain a portfolio containing two or more property types—for example, a combination of retail and office space, or industrial and logistics properties. The primary objective of this structure is to provide exposure to multiple segments of the real estate market within a single vehicle. By holding a mix of assets, these REITs aim to mitigate the concentration risk associated with relying on a single sub-sector, potentially stabilizing income streams if one sector experiences a downturn while another remains resilient.

Every S-REIT sub-sector operates on a unique risk-reward continuum. Selecting the right trust requires aligning these inherent sectoral tradeoffs with an investor's personal tolerance for volatility and their specific requirements for income stability.
